# HOOK SKILL

12 hook types. Each has an explanation, templates, and rules. Templates are the fallback. Find viral posts on the idea first (HOOK SOURCE ORDER). Fill every [slot] from the user's real facts. Never copy a template's example facts. Templates marked (personal) need the user's own real event or number.

## Structure

Every hook has 2 parts:

1. Attention. The claim or fact that makes them stop.
2. Open loop. Promise the answer. Hold it back.

Example: "I create 40 pieces of content per week. Here are my 7 repurposing tactics:"

Rules:

- Max 2 lines. Shorter is fine.
- Write for the phone. The claim must land in the first 140 characters. The "see more" cut is at about 140 characters on mobile and about 210 on desktop.
- After the hook, leave 2 blank lines. The body then drops behind "see more". Keep the blank lines in the final post.
- Specific. Clear numbers. Name the exact thing: "7 repurposing tactics", not "some tips".
- No introduction. No fluff. Straight to the point.
- Can be personal: a real number from the user's own work.
- Personal numbers come from the user only.

## HOOK SOURCE ORDER

Real viral posts first. Templates last.

Step 1. Find similar viral posts.

- Search the web for LinkedIn posts on the user's idea. Use 2 to 3 queries. Example: "LinkedIn post resume skills list mistake", "LinkedIn viral post hiring mistakes".
- Keep a post only if it is on the same idea, from the last 12 months, and shows high reactions or comments for the creator's size. Can't see the numbers? It does not count as viral.
- Keep up to 3 posts.

Step 2. Learn the hook, not the words.

- For each kept post, name the pattern: the type (1-12), the shape of the first line, what it holds back.
- Write a new hook with the same pattern, using only the user's real facts.
- Never copy their words. Never copy their facts or numbers.
- Tell the user which post each hook is based on (creator name and link). The link goes in your reply, never in the post.

Step 3. No match? Use templates.

- Triggers: no web search, no similar viral post, or the posts have no clear hook.
- Say so in one line: "No similar viral post found. Used templates."
- Pick by TEMPLATE PICK below.

Mixed is fine. Example: 1 pattern from a viral post, 2 from templates.

## TEMPLATE PICK

Choose the type that fits the post. Do not choose at random.

| The user gave | Start with |
|---|---|
| A number from their own work | 3 Proof, 9 Surprising fact |
| A dated story or a mistake | 10 Story, 7 Fear |
| A strong opinion | 4 Controversial statement, 8 Emotional agreement |
| A how-to or a list | 2 Value promise |
| Two options or two groups | 11 X vs Y, 5 Unexplained contrast |
| A question the audience argues about | 12 Poll |
| A known name or result they have earned | 1 Credibility jacking |

Then check GOAL FIT. If the two disagree, the goal wins and the material fills the template.
Within a type, pick the template whose slots the user's facts fill with no gaps.

## MATERIAL TEST

Before the hook, check the user gave at least one real thing: a dated moment, a number with the thing it counts, or a position they hold and what it costs them. None? Ask for one. Max 3 questions. Never fill it in.

- Passes: "Lost a client in March 2026."
- Fails: "I learned a lot."

## MODE

Write 3 hooks for the chosen idea, each a different type. Follow HOOK SOURCE ORDER: viral patterns first, templates to fill the rest. Pick types that fit the post goal (GOAL FIT below). Label each hook with its type and its source ("pattern from [creator]" or "template"). Ask the user which to use.

## GOAL FIT

What each goal tends to earn. These are patterns seen in 2026 posts, not a promise.

| Goal | Hook types that tend to fit |
|---|---|
| Comments | 4 Controversial statement, 10 Story, 12 Poll |
| Reposts | 8 Emotional agreement, 11 X vs Y, 4 Controversial statement |
| Likes | 10 Story, 5 Unexplained contrast, 8 Emotional agreement |
| Saves | 2 Value promise, 3 Proof, 9 Surprising fact |

If the user gave no goal, ask once. Still none? Use the idea to decide.

## 1. Credibility jacking

Attach the hook to a person or company the audience already trusts, so their name earns the stop.

Templates:

- "[Name] said [real quote]. Here's why that's wrong."
- "I studied [N] [Name] [things] and here's what I found."
- "Here's the difference between how [Name A] and [Name B] [do thing]."
- "Everyone loves [Name]'s advice on [topic]. Here's why it doesn't apply to you."
- "The late [role] of [Company], [Name], used to [surprising practice]."

Rules:

- Name must be known to this niche, not just famous. AI: Sam Altman, Elon Musk, founders the audience follows. Finance: Warren Buffett, Ray Dalio, Bill Ackman.
- Never invent a quote, claim, or study about a person. Real and checked only.
- A company works too (example: YouTube).
- Tell it as a story. Twist the knife on the surprising or hard-hitting fact.

## 2. Value promise

Name a problem the reader has and promise the fix, so they know exactly what they get.

Templates:

- "[N] [things] that [cause the problem] and how to fix them."
- "[N] [topic] tips you can [do] in [time]."
- "[N] free [things] from [top names]."
- "[N] [things] that will change your [life]."
- "[N] ways to [skill]."
- "[Topic] explained to [simple audience]."
- "How to (actually) [do thing]: a simple guide to [result]."
- "If you want to [big result] ([objection answered, e.g. I'm not kidding]), read this:"
- "[Group] use [thing] to [do X]. Here are [N] [concepts] you need to know:"
- "The most efficient [niche] [framework] for [year]:" then step 1 on the next line.
- "Here's your [N]-week [plan] (to start your [result] in [year]):"
- "[N] reasons to start [thing] in [year]:"
- "This is the winning [niche] [playbook] for [year]. Let me break it down for you with [N] pillars:"
- "If you want [audience] to [goal]: you should first answer these [N] questions:"
- "Here's what you don't need to start [thing]:" then a short list of false needs.
- "[Small input] = [big output]. Here is the [N]-step system to [get it]:"
- "I talk with a lot of [people] who:" then a short list of their shared problems.
- "[Doing X] rewards you [N] times:" then a numbered list, short term first.

Rules:

- Safe and clear, but crowded on LinkedIn. Use only when the topic is new, the visual is unique, or the value is massive. Otherwise combine with another type.
- Visual idea: problem in red, fix in green.
- Big claims: answer the reader's first objection ("is this hyperbole?") inside the hook.
- A well-known brand or school in the hook adds credibility.

## 3. Proof

Open with real evidence of why the reader should listen: credentials, data, or results.

Templates:

- "I've analyzed [N] [things]."
- "I grew from [N] to [N] [followers]."
- "I was a [real past role]. Here's what it taught me about [topic]."
- "I've got the receipts: [real proof]."
- "I've trained [N]+ [people] to [do thing]. Most struggled with the SAME [problem]:"
- "In the last [N] years, over [N] people have asked me [question]."
- "In [year], I had [low number]. Today [real result]. Here's how I got there:" (personal)
- "This week we published a viral [thing] ([N] impressions) on [topic]. Here are the [N] reasons why it worked:" (personal)
- "[Group] struggle to [do thing] in [year]. I've audited [N] of them, here are the top [N] problems:" (personal)
- "I've coached over [N] [people] in the past [time]. The market is moving, here are [N] priorities to focus on:" (personal)
- "My [business] made over [real result] from [thing]. Here are the [N] types of [thing] for [goal]:" (personal)
- "I've audited over [N] [things]. My advice: ask yourself THIS question:" (personal)
- "My [thing] just hit [real milestone]. [N] rules I wish I knew when I started in [year]:" (personal)
- "I've invested over [N] hours in [field]. Here are my [N] golden rules to [goal]:" (personal)
- "I've been a [role] for [N] years. Here's the biggest [problem] I've seen:" (personal)
- "I've helped over [N] [clients] in the past [time]. In [year], I see [group] make the biggest mistake:" (personal)
- "I've been doing [field] for [N] years. These [N] concepts made me a better [role]:" (personal)
- "Every week, [group] come to me with this problem:" then their quote. (personal)
- "I've helped over [N] [clients] with [field]. Here are the [N] rules to build your [result]:" (personal)
- "In [month, year], a lot of [people] come to me saying "[their complaint]". When I audit their [thing], I usually spot this ONE major problem:" (personal)

Rules:

- Real receipts only. Never invent one.
- Receipts to list: past roles, companies, clients helped, data analyzed, frameworks created.
- Show proof in text and visual. Example: credentials plus a photo in uniform.

## 4. Controversial statement

Challenge a common belief so the reader wants to see how it resolves.

Templates:

- "Everyone tells you to [common advice]. That's terrible advice. Here's why."
- "[Name] with [N] followers is wrong."
- "[People] don't [common thing]. [What really happens]."
- "I'm frustrated by the [topic] [things] floating around."
- "[N] [subjects or things] that should be mandatory in [institution]."
- [Role]: "[common phrase]." Then: "This is the worst [niche] mindset for [N] reasons:"
- "Good news: the [quality bar] for [niche] is low in [year]. That means it's easier to [goal]:"
- "Too many [group] start from "[common approach]". Here are the [N] [things] they're missing:"
- "Too many [group] [do wrong thing] and call it "[good-sounding term]"."
- '"[Common belief]" Good in theory, but here are [N] problems:'
- '"[Common demand]" These expectations come from one false belief:'
- "Every [group] wants to [goal]. But most of them fall into this ONE trap:"
- "Many [group] confuse [A] with [B]:" then a short list of how.

Rules:

- Write the post first. Find its most contrarian line. Move it to the hook.
- Must be controversial enough to be interesting.
- The post must truly argue it.
- Naming a person is also type 1. Apply type 1 rules.
- Tap a frustration many people already share.

## 5. Unexplained contrast

Text and visual clash, leaving a question the reader clicks "see more" to answer.

Templates:

- "I procrastinated for [N] years on the [ordinal] most important decision of my life." Visual: matching photo.
- "I used to struggle with [thing] and now I've achieved [result]." Visual: proof, for example you on stage.
- "I used to get [N] likes on my posts." Visual: analytics screenshot with big numbers.

Rules:

- Needs the user's real story and real visual.
- Text and visual should say opposite things.

## 6. Visual

Make the post look unlike the feed, because novelty stops the scroll.

Templates:

- "[Unusual style] instead of [common style]." Examples: blobs and smileys instead of a typical carousel. Pink instead of blue. A caricature instead of a stock photo.
- "[Plain quote] on a bold, unusual background color."
- "[Handwritten page, candid selfie, or smiling face] with the hook text."

Rules:

- This skill writes text. Add a one-line visual idea.
- Inspiration: Say What content database, Pinterest carousel and infographic layouts.
- Lo-fi photos feel real. A face builds familiarity.

## 7. Fear

Name the cost of a mistake or a missed chance, because people avoid pain more than they chase gain.

Templates:

- "Brutal but true [things] that can hurt your [career]."
- "[N] mistakes killing your [result]."
- "[Topic] advice that kills your chance of [goal]."
- "Stop doing [thing] or you'll never [goal]."
- "[Good thing] isn't enough anymore."
- "[N] [habits or things] you must break to become your best [self]."
- "In [year], [group] have developed a [new defense]. So now, you can't:"
- "Biggest mistake I see in [niche] [topic]:" then "(Read this to avoid [cost] in [year])"
- "Your [thing] needs [X]. Otherwise, this problem will happen:"
- "Your [thing] will plateau in [year]. Unless you add these [N] [tactics] to [result]:"

Rules:

- The cost must be true.
- Put the cost in the text hook or the visual title.
- Example visual: a person eaten by a snake. Graphic, so "brutal" lands.

## 8. Emotional agreement

State a belief people feel in the gut and agree with at once. Liking it tells their network "this is what I believe."

Templates:

- "Surround yourself with people who [do thing] in rooms you're not in."
- "Your [worth] isn't defined by your [job title]."
- "[Taking care of X] isn't [negative word], it's necessary."
- "Stop waiting for permission to [change careers]."
- "A good [role] can significantly improve your [life]."
- "[Value] matters."
- "We become [identity] the day we decide to [do X], not [Y]."
- "Newsflash: nobody starts [at the top]."
- "PSA: [N] years from now, the only people who will remember [thing] are [people]."
- "This [source] post hit me hard. A few of the responses:"
- "[Do X]. Anything. Stop [Y]. Just [do it]." (a wake-up call with no lead-in)

Rules:

- Hit emotion, not intellect.
- LinkedIn is not anonymous. People share what they want colleagues to see.
- A reframe that hits a gut feeling works best: "Maybe I've got it all wrong?"

## 9. Surprising fact

Open with a surprising fact or number that makes the reader question their own situation.

Templates:

- "I paid [someone] $[X] to [small task] in [time]. Said they had [N] [jobs] that day. Do the math. Have you made $[total] in one day?" (personal)
- "[N]% of [thing] worldwide is [lost or wasted]. That's about [big concrete number]."
- "[Big brand] published (and soon deleted) [thing]."
- "If your [business] was worth [amount], would you donate [amount] to [cause]?"
- '[Year] is the "[coined term]" of [niche]. Let's rewind [N] years to see where it's coming from:'

Rules:

- Real, sourced facts only. Check the math.
- Pair the fact with a fear of being outdone when it fits.
- Question hook: use only after a statement line, never as the first line.

## 10. Story

Open with a real moment from the user's work or life. Leave the result open.

Templates:

- "WOW! Today was the most surreal day of my career. [Famous name]'s team asked me to [do thing]." (personal)
- "Day [N] at [Company]. [Time] ago, [person] and I [did X] near [dream place]." (personal)
- "I once [surprising decision] [time] into [event]. [Surprising result]. Lesson learned:" (personal)
- "I [bold decision] with [counterintuitive condition]..." (personal)
- "This was the EXACT moment I knew [realization]." (personal)
- '[Role]: "[their line]" Me: "[start of your answer]:"' (personal)
- "[N] weeks ago I had dinner with [N] [group]. I noticed [N] traits they all shared:" (personal)
- "We went from [start] to [result] in [time]. But for a long time, I made one mistake that cost me a lot:" (personal)
- "I've just arrived in [place], and I'm looking to meet: [list of who]" (personal)

Rules:

- Real events only. Never invent one.
- Celebration posts win "congrats." A humble brag works when it also shows credibility.
- Leave the outcome open so they click "see more."

## 11. X vs Y

A short hook plus a list. The reader wants to see what is being compared to what.

Templates:

- "Nobody will remember: [list]. People will remember: [list]."
- "Bad [role] [do X]. Good [role] [do Y]. Great [role] [do Z]."
- "[Thing] "plan" vs what actually happens:" Visual: a meme or funny image.
- "[A] and [B] have one BIG difference:" then "(Yet many [group] think they're "similar")"

Rules:

- Pick a common enemy the audience shares.
- Keep lists short.

## 12. Poll

Ask one question the audience answers in one tap. Each vote is engagement, and the result gives you a follow-up post.

Templates:

- "Have you [done the trending thing] for [task]?" Options: "Yes, already using it" / "Yes, building it now" / "Not yet, going to" / "No, not going to".
- "[Which one] matters more for [goal]?" Options: [A] / [B] / [C] / Other.

Rules:

- The question is the post text. Keep it to one line.
- Give 3 to 4 options. Cover every honest answer.
- Pick a topic the audience cares about right now.
- Never invent poll results. Report the real result in a follow-up post.

## Combo

Layer 2 or more types in one hook for a stronger pattern interrupt.

Templates:

- Proof + controversy: "I analyzed [N] [things] and found that [common belief] is wrong."
- Contrast + fear: "I used to make this mistake and it cost me [real cost]."
- Value promise + visual: "[N] [topic] tips you can implement in [time]" plus a novel visual.
- Fear + value promise + emotion: "Brutal truths about [topic]:" with points like "Hard work isn't enough."

Rules:

- Start with one type. Layer more as it gets easy.
